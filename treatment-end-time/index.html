Add the time, leave decisions to the unit

This dialysis treatment end time calculator answers a clock question: if a run starts at the time entered and lasts the duration entered, what time is that on the clock? It does not select a duration or say that a treatment is complete. Enter a start time, whole hours and any additional minutes. The result is a planned clock label based only on those entries. Use it for arithmetic and organization, with your unit's orders and policy taking priority.

Try a simple example

A start of 06:00 plus four hours gives 10:00. A start of 07:45 plus three hours and thirty minutes gives 11:15. One way to check the second example is to add the whole hours first, reaching 10:45, then add thirty minutes. Fifteen minutes reaches 11:00 and the remaining fifteen reaches 11:15. Keeping hours and minutes in separate fields avoids confusing three hours thirty minutes with the decimal number 3.30 hours.

When a run passes midnight

The clock returns to 00:00 after 23:59, but elapsed time continues. Starting at 22:30 and adding three hours thirty minutes gives 02:00 the next day. This page marks the day rollover so an early-morning clock label is not mistaken for an earlier time on the starting day. For longer arithmetic durations, it reports the number of days crossed. It does not attach a calendar date to the answer. If a schedule needs dates, record those separately using your usual system.

Understand the clock convention

The displayed answer uses a 24-hour clock: 13:00 is 1 p.m., while 01:00 is 1 a.m. Your device may show the time input with an AM/PM picker, but the calculated label stays in 24-hour form. The calculator converts the duration to minutes, adds it to the start's minutes since midnight, and wraps the answer at 1,440 minutes per day. Enter hours as whole numbers and additional minutes from zero through fifty-nine. At least one minute of duration is required.

Limits worth keeping in view

This page has no date or time-zone field. It therefore performs ordinary clock arithmetic, not a daylight-saving-aware elapsed-time calculation. A clock change can make a real elapsed duration differ from simple wall-clock addition. Travel between time zones is also outside its scope. Interruptions, late starts and revised durations are not tracked automatically. If an input changes, the result changes, but that does not update any record or equipment. The separate shift tools page offers a countdown tied to today's local start time; that is a different display with different assumptions.

A quick cross-check

Check the start's AM/PM meaning, read the hours and minutes separately, and confirm any next-day label. Keep patient details out of the page. No entries are sent to a server, and this calculator is not a treatment log.

Frequently asked questions

What happens when the end time crosses midnight?

The calculator wraps the clock to the next day and labels the number of days crossed. For example, 22:30 plus 3 hours 30 minutes is 02:00 the next day.

Does this page include pauses or delays?

No. It adds the duration entered to the start time entered. It has no information about actual starts, interruptions or completion.

Does this calculator account for daylight saving time?

No. This page uses clock arithmetic without a date or time zone. The separate shift tools page has a date-based countdown for today.
