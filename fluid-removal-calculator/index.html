What the numbers mean

This dialysis fluid removal calculator keeps one small piece of arithmetic in one place. You supply a pre-weight, a target weight and a run time. It subtracts the target from the pre-weight, displays the difference in kilograms, multiplies that difference by 1,000 to show a milliliter equivalent, then divides that amount by the hours entered. It does not determine a target, write an order or decide what should happen during a treatment.

A worked example, not a recommendation

With a pre-weight of 72.5 kg and a target weight of 70 kg, subtraction gives 2.5 kg. Using the tool's water-equivalent arithmetic, 2.5 × 1,000 gives 2,500 mL. Dividing by a four-hour run gives 625 mL/hr. Those are example inputs to demonstrate subtraction, multiplication and division. They are not suggested settings for any person. The mL/hr result is an average across the entered duration, not a machine instruction or a safety assessment.

Keep the units straight

Both weights must be in kilograms. Mixing pounds in one box with kilograms in the other makes the subtraction meaningless. The run-time box uses decimal hours: four hours thirty minutes is 4.5 hours, not 4.30. To convert minutes to a fraction of an hour, divide them by 60 before adding the whole hours. Fifteen minutes is 0.25 hours; forty-five minutes is 0.75 hours. Check the unit printed beside each field before entering a number.

Know what is left out

A kilogram is a unit of mass and a milliliter is a unit of volume. The 1 kg to 1,000 mL step here is a simplified water-equivalent convention, not a universal conversion for every material. A difference between two scale readings is not, by itself, a complete accounting of fluid. This page does not include other inputs, outputs, allowances, scale conditions or changes during the run. It also does not calculate a weight-normalized rate. Use your unit's ordered method rather than treating this narrow result as a complete UF goal.

Check before copying

Read the supplied weights back, including the decimal point, and confirm the duration. A zero difference displays zero; a negative difference is flagged as pre-weight below target. Neither result tells you what action to take. Blank fields, non-numeric entries and non-positive weights or duration cannot produce a result. The display rounds the weight difference to three decimal places and milliliters and milliliters per hour to whole numbers. Keep the original values for any required independent check instead of repeatedly converting rounded answers.

Frequently asked questions

Does this calculator choose a UF goal?

No. It subtracts two supplied weights and divides by a supplied duration. Orders and unit policy determine what is appropriate.

What does a negative difference mean?

The entered pre-weight is lower than the entered target weight. The page flags that difference; it does not recommend a fluid-removal action.

Can I add other fluid amounts?

This tool has no fields for other fluid amounts. It only shows the arithmetic from the two weights and run time you enter.
