Same quantity, different units

This nurse unit converter handles three everyday pairs: pounds and kilograms, Fahrenheit and Celsius, and milliliters and US fluid ounces. Type in either box of a pair and its partner updates. The conversion changes the unit used to describe a quantity; it does not decide whether a value is appropriate. Use original measurements, check the unit labels and follow required independent verification. This is a small arithmetic aid, not a dosing tool or a record of care.

Pounds to kilograms

One international avoirdupois pound equals exactly 0.45359237 kilograms. Multiply pounds by that factor to obtain kilograms; divide kilograms by the same factor to go the other way. For example, 220 lb displays as 99.790 kg, or 99.79 when trailing zeros are omitted. A value of 100 kg displays as 220.462 lb. These displays are rounded to three decimal places. The pound here is a unit of mass, not a fluid measure. Do not substitute this pair for a volume conversion.

Fahrenheit to Celsius

Temperature conversion uses an offset as well as a scale factor. To convert Fahrenheit to Celsius, subtract 32, then multiply by five and divide by nine. To convert Celsius to Fahrenheit, multiply by nine, divide by five, then add 32. For a familiar arithmetic check, 98.6 °F becomes 37 °C. Another useful check is that −40 is the same in both scales. Answers display to two decimal places. These formulas do not classify a temperature or give advice about what to do with it.

Milliliters to US fluid ounces

One US fluid ounce equals 29.5735295625 mL. Divide milliliters by that number to get US fluid ounces; multiply fluid ounces by it to get milliliters. Thus 1,000 mL displays as 33.814 US fl oz, and 8 US fl oz displays as 236.59 mL. Fluid ounces measure volume. Ounces on a scale measure mass and are not interchangeable with them. Imperial fluid ounces use a different factor, so confirm which system a container or source uses before copying a result.

Rounding and repeat conversions

Keep the original value when accuracy matters. A displayed answer is rounded, so sending it back through the opposite box may not reproduce every digit of the original. That is a display-precision issue, not a new measurement. Milliliters display to two decimal places and fluid ounces to three. Blank inputs clear the paired field; a non-numeric entry cannot generate an answer. Zero is a valid arithmetic input. Negative temperatures are also valid, but a mathematical conversion does not validate the meaning of any entered measurement.

Use the right source value

Read the original unit before typing. Avoid adding names, dates of birth or other identifying details: none are needed for these conversions. Calculations happen locally after the page loads, with no login or tracking scripts. Opening or reloading still needs a connection. If you need inches and centimeters too, the linked shift tools page includes that extra pair. Follow your unit's documentation convention rather than assuming the page's displayed precision is the required precision.

Frequently asked questions

Which ounces does this converter use?

US fluid ounces, a volume unit. It does not convert to ounces of weight or Imperial fluid ounces.

Why does a round trip sometimes change the last digit?

Displayed answers are rounded. Converting an already rounded answer back can lose a small amount of precision.

Can these conversions decide a dose?

No. They only change units. They do not interpret a label, select a dose or replace required independent checks.
